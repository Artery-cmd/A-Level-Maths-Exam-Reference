Paper 1 checklist

Measurements & Errors

  • Absolute, fractional and percentage uncertainty — know all three forms
  • Adding quantities: add absolute uncertainties
  • Multiplying/dividing: add percentage uncertainties
  • Powers: multiply % uncertainty by the power
  • Random error: spread of results around mean. Systematic: constant offset
  • Precision ≠ accuracy. Repeatable ≠ reproducible
  • Log graphs: ln y = n ln x + ln A (power law); ln y = kx + ln A (exponential)
  • Gradient uncertainty: draw steepest and shallowest lines through error bars

Particles & Radiation

  • Proton = uud, Neutron = udd
  • Up quark: Q=+⅔, B=⅓. Down quark: Q=−⅓, B=⅓
  • β⁻: n→p+e⁻+ν̄ₑ (W⁻ boson). β⁺: p→n+e⁺+νₑ (W⁺ boson)
  • Conservation laws: charge, baryon number, lepton number — check all three
  • Strangeness: conserved in strong, can change ±1 in weak interactions
  • Strong force: attractive 0.5–3 fm, repulsive below 0.5 fm, zero above ~3 fm
  • Photoelectric: hf = φ + ½mv²max. No electrons below threshold frequency
  • de Broglie: λ = h/mv — NOT in booklet
  • Energy levels: photon energy = difference between two levels = hf

Waves

  • v = fλ. EM waves: v = c = 3×10⁸ ms⁻¹ in vacuum
  • Stationary waves: nodes = zero amplitude, antinodes = max amplitude
  • Formed by superposition of two identical waves travelling in opposite directions
  • Diffraction grating: d sinθ = nλ. More slits → sharper, brighter maxima
  • Young's slits: λ = ax/D (a = slit sep, x = fringe width, D = distance)
  • Refractive index: n = sin i / sin r = c/v. Critical angle: sin C = 1/n
  • Polarisation is evidence that EM waves are transverse

Mechanics & Materials

  • Impulse = FΔt = Δ(mv). Area under F-t graph = impulse
  • Momentum always conserved in closed system
  • Elastic collision: KE conserved. Inelastic: KE not conserved
  • Hooke's law: F = kx up to elastic limit only
  • Stress = F/A, Strain = ΔL/L, Young modulus E = stress/strain
  • Elastic strain energy = ½Fx = ½kx² = area under F-x graph
  • Projectile: horizontal (constant v) and vertical (a=g) are independent

Electricity

  • Kirchhoff 1: ΣI in = ΣI out at any junction
  • Kirchhoff 2: ΣV = ΣEMF around any closed loop
  • Internal resistance: ε = V + Ir. Terminal p.d. drops under load
  • Potential divider: Vout = Vin × R₂/(R₁+R₂)
  • Resistivity: R = ρL/A. ρ increases with temperature for metals
  • I-V graphs: ohmic (straight line), filament lamp (curves), diode (threshold ~0.6V)

Further Mechanics

  • Circular motion: v=ωr, a=v²/r=ω²r, centripetal F=mv²/r towards centre
  • SHM condition: a = −ω²x (restoring, proportional to displacement)
  • x=Acosωt, v=−Aω sinωt, vmax=Aω at x=0, amax=Aω² at x=±A
  • T=2π√(m/k) for spring; T=2π√(l/g) for pendulum — NOT in booklet
  • Resonance: max amplitude when driving freq = natural freq. Damping reduces peak
  • Ideal gas: pV=nRT. Mean KE of molecule = 3/2 kT
  • Q=mcΔT (specific heat capacity), Q=mL (specific latent heat)
Paper 2 checklist

Thermal Physics

  • Ideal gas: pV=nRT. Mean KE of molecule = 3/2 kT
  • Q=mcΔT (specific heat capacity), Q=mL (specific latent heat)
  • Kinetic Theory Assumptions: Elastic Collision, Random Motion, Negligible Volume, etc
  • Internal Energy = Kinetic + Potential; Temperature = Measure of Kinetic Energy
  • Deriving Kinetic Theory Equation: pV = 1/3Nm ⟨c2⟩
  • Using n=N/NA=m/M and k=R/NA; Brownian motion &mdash Random motion of particles due to surrounding particles

Fields

  • Gravitational: F=GMm/r², g=GM/r², V=−GM/r (always negative)
  • Electric: F=kQq/r², E=kQ/r², V=kQ/r (can be positive or negative)
  • Field lines always perpendicular to equipotentials. E=−dV/dr
  • Capacitance C=Q/V. Parallel plates: C=ε₀εᵣA/d
  • Time constant: τ=RC. Graph: Charging - t at 63%. Discharging - t at 37%
  • Magnetic force: F=BIl, F=Bqv Where B ⟂ ⟂ I ⟂ ⟂ F
  • Faraday: EMF=−NdΦ/dt. Lenz: induced current opposes the change that creates it

Nuclear Physics

  • α decay: A−4, Z−2. β⁻: Z+1. β⁺: Z−1. γ: no change to A or Z
  • A=λN, λ=ln2/t½, N=N₀e^(−λt) — λ=ln2/t½
  • Binding energy = mass defect × c². Higher BE/nucleon = more stable
  • Fission: heavy nucleus splits. Fusion: light nuclei combine. Both release energy
  • Products always have Higher BE/nucleon. Graph: Lighter than Iron - Fusion. Heavier - Fission
  • 1 eV = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹J. 1 u = 1.661×10⁻²⁷ kg = 931.5 MeV/c². Mass of reactants > Mass of Product
  • Which Decay: β⁺: Proton Rich. γ: Excited. β⁻: Neutron rich. α: Nucleon rich
Paper 3 — practicals (Section A)

Practical skills

  • Sums/Differences: Add absolute uncertainties. Products/quotients: Add fractional/percentage uncertainties
  • Power law y=Axⁿ: plot ln y vs ln x → gradient = n, y-intercept = ln A
  • Exponential y=Ae^(kx): plot ln y vs x → gradient = k, y-intercept = ln A
  • Uncertainty in gradient: steepest and shallowest lines through all error bars
  • State independent, dependent and control variables for every experiment
  • Anomalous results: plot but exclude from best-fit line, state why
  • Resolution ≠ uncertainty: Uncertainty ≈ ½ × smallest scale division
  • Measurement vs Reading: Measurement: uncertainty x 2.
Paper 3 — Option (Section B)

Astrophysics

  • Luminosity: L=4πr²σT⁴ (Stefan's law). Peak wavelength: λmaxT=2.9×10⁻³ m K (Wien)
  • Standard candles: Cepheid variables and Type 1a supernovae
  • Hubble's law: v=H₀d. Age of universe ≈ 1/H₀ ≈ 13.8 billion years
  • Redshift: z=(λobs−λrest)/λrest = v/c for v<<c
  • HR diagram: main sequence top-left (hot/bright) to bottom-right (cool/dim)

SHM & circular motion
Simple Harmonic Motion
a = −ω²x (defining equation — memorise) x = A cosωt    v = −Aω sinωt vmax = Aω (at x = 0) amax = Aω² (at x = ±A) T = 2π√(m/k)    T = 2π√(l/g)
KE max at centre (x=0), PE max at extremes (x=±A). Total energy = ½mω²A² = constant
Circular Motion
v = ωr    T = 2π/ω a = v²/r = ω²r (centripetal, towards centre) F = mv²/r = mω²r
Centripetal force is not a separate force — it's the resultant of real forces (tension, gravity, normal reaction etc.) directed towards the centre
Projectile motion
Horizontal and vertical are independent
Horizontal (constant v): x = v cosθ · t
Vertical (a = g = 9.81): y = v sinθ · t − ½gt² vy = v sinθ − gt
At max height: vertical velocity = 0. Time of flight: solve y=0. Range: x at that time

Data analysis techniques
Linearising graphs
Power law: y = Axⁿ → ln y = n ln x + ln A (plot ln y vs ln x, gradient = n) Exponential: y = Ae^(kx) → ln y = kx + ln A (plot ln y vs x, gradient = k)
Uncertainty rules
Add/subtract: add ABSOLUTE uncertainties Multiply/divide: add % uncertainties Power n: multiply % uncertainty by n Gradient: Δm = (m_max − m_min)/2
Resolution of instrument ≈ half the smallest scale division. State it explicitly
⚠ Practical exam traps
Log graph gradient: ln y vs ln x → gradient = power n. ln y vs x → gradient = −λ or k  ·  Anomalous points: still plot them, just don't include in best-fit line — state they were excluded  ·  Uncertainty not resolution: uncertainty in a reading is typically ±½ smallest division  ·  Control variables: must be able to name at least 2–3 for any experiment asked about  ·  Required practicals in context: Section A questions often describe an unfamiliar version — apply the same principles
Physics command words
State: one-line fact, no explanation  ·  Explain: give the mechanism — WHY not just WHAT  ·  Describe: say what happens including direction/magnitude  ·  Show that: show all working clearly — never work backwards  ·  Calculate: substitution, working, answer, units  ·  Suggest: apply known physics principles to an unfamiliar context
